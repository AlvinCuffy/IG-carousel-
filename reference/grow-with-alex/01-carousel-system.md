> THREE COLOURS. TWO FONTS. ONE LAYOUT.

## Why carousels get scrolled past

Most carousels don't fail on content. They fail because they look average. Five colours fight each other, three random fonts show up, and the layout changes on every slide. The feed reads that as noise and the thumb keeps moving.

Carousels that get saved look like one brand, not six. That comes from three decisions made once: colour, then fonts, then layout. Lock them and every future post starts almost designed before you've typed a word.

## The system

### Rule 01 — Colour

Colour is the first thing the grid sees and the first thing that sinks a post when it's wrong. The fix isn't nicer colours, it's fewer of them, each with a fixed job: a primary, a background and one accent.

- **The trap:** letting the background image smuggle in extra colours. A photo with a strong blue and orange already uses your three. Add a purple headline and a yellow badge and now five colours are fighting.
- **The fix:** pick three and give each a role. Primary is your brand colour. Background is the neutral most of the canvas sits on. Accent is one colour, used sparingly, for the thing you want seen.
- **The check:** count the colours on your cover. More than three with a real job each, and you cut until only three remain.

### Rule 02 — Fonts

Two fonts, two jobs. One shouts to stop the scroll. One stays quiet so the words can be read.

- **The trap:** a body font with personality. If your paragraph text is trying to be interesting, it's fighting the headline and costing you the read.
- **The fix:** one display font, bold and full of character, for headlines. One clean sans, almost invisible, for body. Lock both and never choose again.
- **The check:** cover the headline. If the body font still pulls your eye, it's too loud. Swap it for something plainer.

### Rule 03 — Layout

Layout is the decision you make once and genuinely never make again. A fixed home for every element: headline, body, handle, page number, arrow.

- **The trap:** rebuilding the layout every post. A new arrangement each time is exactly why a feed looks like six different people posting.
- **The fix:** sketch one slide. Lock where the headline, body, handle and arrow live. That sketch is your template for every carousel from now on.
- **The check:** open your last three covers side by side. Do the elements sit in the same place on each? If not, pick one layout and hold it.

### Bonus — The Grid Test

People don't judge a single slide, they tap your profile and read the whole grid in about two seconds. Zoom out on your last nine posts. If the first three rows look intentional, you have a brand. If they look random, no single post is good enough to save it. This is also what makes you fast: once you can see the grid, you already know what the next post needs.

## The prompts

Run the system through Claude with the Higgsfield connector on. Build inside one chat, feeding each result back as the reference for the next, so the look compounds instead of resetting.

### Prompt 1 — Let AI pick your system

```
I'm building a carousel brand. Give me 3 colour palettes, each with a primary, a background and one accent, with hex codes.
Then give me 2 font pairings, one heavy display font for headlines and one clean sans for body.
Keep it minimal. No more than 3 colours per palette.
```

### Prompt 2 — Build it in order

```
Step 1, colour: Create a carousel cover background using only these 3 colours: [primary], [background], [accent]. Accent used sparingly. Clean and minimal. No text yet.
Step 2, fonts: Using that as the reference, add a headline reading "[your headline]" in a heavy display font. Keep smaller text in a clean sans. Two fonts only.
Step 3, layout: Keep the same colours and fonts. Headline in the top zone, @[handle] small in the bottom corner, a small forward arrow on the right edge, generous margins. This is my fixed template.
```

### Prompt 3 — The all-in-one

```
Apply my full system in one go.
COLOUR: only [primary], [background] and [accent]. Accent sparingly.
FONTS: heavy display font for the headline, clean sans for everything small. Two fonts.
LAYOUT: headline "[your headline]" in the top zone, @[handle] small in the bottom corner, small forward arrow on the right edge, generous margins.
```

## The pre-flight checklist

Before you post any cover, run these seven checks. One "no" is the difference between a save and a scroll-past.

- [ ] Colour: three colours only, each with a job. Primary, background, accent.
- [ ] Image: the background isn't smuggling in extra colours.
- [ ] Fonts: two only. One display, one clean body.
- [ ] Body: the body font has zero personality.
- [ ] Layout: headline, handle and arrow sit in their locked positions.
- [ ] Cover: it stops the scroll on its own, with no caption for context.
- [ ] Grid: it still looks on-brand next to your last eight posts.
