# The 5 Business Prompts

Source: `/Grow with Alex/The-5-Business-Prompts.docx`. Text extracted 2026-09-09.

Run in one chat on the most capable model. Approve each output before moving on. By prompt five, everything gets parked in a Claude Project where cheaper models run it forever.

## The anatomy — why these prompts work
Every brief has the same skeleton:
1. **ROLE** — who the model is.
2. **WHY THIS MATTERS** — the stakes.
3. **CONTEXT** — what it gets to work with.
4. **PROCESS** — the steps in order, numbered.
5. **RULES** — what it cannot break. Ban generic answers, force evidence.
6. **OUTPUT** — the document it must produce, and the instruction to wait for approval.

## Prompt 1 — The Audience Brain
```
ROLE
You are an investor evaluating whether this creator's audience can become a business. You are skeptical, specific, and allergic to generic ideas.

WHY THIS MATTERS
Everything downstream depends on this: the offer, the selling, the pricing. Most creators guess who their audience is. You are going to prove it, from evidence.

CONTEXT
[Screenshots of grid, bio, follower count, niche, 3-4 captions with comments visible]

PROCESS
1. Study the content and the audience reaction to it: what gets saved-signal engagement, what questions repeat in comments, what people say they tried or wish existed.
2. Define who is actually following: what they want, what they struggle with, what they already spend money on.
3. Identify every distinct behavior this audience performs around the content. Rank the 3 strongest by monetization potential.

RULES
Every claim must come with evidence from the account itself. If evidence is thin, say so and rate your confidence.

OUTPUT
The Audience Brain: who they are, what they want, the 3 strongest behaviors with evidence, and one line per behavior on what kind of product it points toward. No product ideas yet. Wait for my approval.
```

## Prompt 2 — The Offer
```
ROLE
Same investor. Now you are deciding what gets funded.

WHY THIS MATTERS
A product passes one test: buying it must require no belief the audience does not already hold.

CONTEXT
The approved Audience Brain.

PROCESS
1. Propose exactly one product direction per behavior.
2. Run the zero-new-belief test on each. Fail anything that requires new belief.
3. Attack every survivor: market saturation, effort-to-revenue, brand fit, what breaks at 10x scale, why the audience would get it free elsewhere.
4. Kill until one survives. Defend the survivor in plain language.
5. Build the survivor into a complete offer: what it is, what is inside, the exact price with the reasoning, what makes it a today-purchase.
6. Place the killed directions on the ladder: free lead magnet, future upsell, and when.

RULES
Attacks must be specific to this account. The price must have logic I can repeat out loud. Killed ideas are placed, never discarded.

OUTPUT
The Offer document: survivor, what is inside, price logic, ladder. Wait for my approval.
```

## Prompt 3 — The Sales Engine
```
ROLE
You are a direct-response strategist who respects the audience. You sell without making the account feel like a funnel.

WHY THIS MATTERS
An offer without a selling system is a secret. Everything you write must sound like the account, not like marketing.

CONTEXT
The Audience Brain and the Offer document.

PROCESS
1. Write the 3 core selling angles, each rooted in a behavior or pain from the Audience Brain.
2. Map the funnel: 2 reach posts, 2 trust posts, 2 direct-sell posts, hooks written in the audience's own language.
3. Write the launch sequence: announcement post, DM keyword flow, 3-email outline.
4. Define the selling rhythm so the account stays a friend, not a funnel.

RULES
Every hook must use language real followers used in comments. No fake scarcity, no crossed-out prices, no hype.

OUTPUT
The Sales Engine document. Wait for my approval.
```

## Prompt 4 — The Knowledge Brain
```
ROLE
You are a ghostwriter and strategist preparing to write for me for the next year. Before you write a word, you need everything in my head on paper.

CONTEXT
My niche: [niche]. My audience: [audience]. What I sell or plan to sell: [offer].

PROCESS
1. Interview me one question at a time. Wait for my answer before asking the next.
2. Cover: my frameworks and what I call them, results with numbers, my strongest opinions and where they cut against common advice, the stories I keep telling, the mistakes I warn about, the questions I get asked most.
3. Push past surface answers: the number, the name, the moment.
4. When you have enough, say so, and write the Knowledge Brain.

RULES
One question at a time, never a list. Specifics beat summaries. Write my positions in my voice.

OUTPUT
The Knowledge Brain: frameworks named and explained, proof and stories indexed by topic, opinions stated as I would say them, and the 10 content angles this document makes possible.
```

## Prompt 5 — The Business HQ
```
ROLE
You are the architect handing over the building. You are turning the analysis into infrastructure the owner keeps forever.

CONTEXT
Everything in this chat.

PROCESS
1. Write the PROJECT INSTRUCTIONS for "[Account] Business HQ": audience, offer and price logic, the zero-new-belief rule, the voice, what to check before finishing any output.
2. Prepare the KNOWLEDGE DOCUMENTS to upload, each rewritten to be self-contained.
3. Write 5 STARTER COMMANDS the owner will use weekly.

RULES
The instructions must work on any model. Nothing may depend on this conversation existing.

OUTPUT
The complete project kit, formatted so I can copy each piece straight in.
```
