# The Brand Brain Kit — Master Brand Brain

Source: `/Grow with Alex/Master_Brand_Brain_Kit.docx`. Text extracted 2026-09-09.

The Claude project that makes every branding decision for you, forever. Four pillars — Positioning, Voice, Visual Identity, Story — built once inside a Claude project and called back into every piece of content from that point forward.

Work through it in order. Step 1 gets raw material out of your head. Step 2 sets up the project. Step 3 chains five prompts to build the brain. Step 4 shows how to use it. Don't skip Step 1 — the quality of the brain is the quality of the raw material.

## Step 1 — Gather the raw material

Five categories. Be specific. Specificity is the whole game.

### 1. Your audience — who you're for, and who you're not
1. Who is this person? Age, role, stage of business.
2. What do they already know? What do they not know?
3. What do they want — at the surface level, and underneath.
4. What do they fear, resist, or roll their eyes at.
5. Who is this not for. Be honest. The wrong audience kills a brand.

### 2. Mission and beliefs — what you stand for, and what you're against
1. What is broken about how this work is currently being done?
2. What do most people in your space get wrong?
3. What do you believe that would make people uncomfortable?
4. What's the change you want to make in your audience's life?
5. What's the change you want to make in the wider industry?

### 3. Voice samples — how you actually sound
Don't describe your voice. Show it. Paste 5–10 samples: captions, emails, DMs, transcripts, voice notes transcribed verbatim, replies where you got fired up, and a few lines you'd never say.

### 4. Visual reference — how it should look and feel
1. Three to five colours you keep coming back to. Hex codes if you have them.
2. Two fonts that feel like you. One serif, one sans, usually.
3. Three reference brands you admire. What specifically do you love about each?
4. The mood in one sentence. "Quiet luxury," "underground cinema," "clinic precision," "vintage Japanese film."
5. What this isn't. Maximalist? Cute? Corporate? Name what you'd refuse.

### 5. Story and through-line
1. What did you do before this. What changed.
2. The lesson you keep coming back to in your work.
3. The one sentence that summarises why this brand exists.
4. The receipt — proof you've lived this.

Drop each category into its own short document. Upload as project knowledge in Step 2.

## Step 2 — Set up the Claude project

Create a project called Master Brand Brain. Upload the five documents. Paste this as the project instructions:

```
You are the Master Brand Brain for [YOUR BRAND NAME].

You have access to five raw material documents in project knowledge:
1. Audience  2. Mission and beliefs  3. Voice samples  4. Visual reference  5. Story.

From this point on, every conversation in this project is grounded in those documents.
Pull from them. Do not invent. Do not generalise.
If something is missing, ask — do not fill the gap with a generic answer.

We will build the brain in four pillars, in this order:
1. Positioning  2. Voice  3. Visual Identity  4. Story.

Each pillar gets its own document, saved into project knowledge once approved.
Each pillar references the ones already built.
Output should be specific, opinionated, and direct. No hedging. No padding.

Wait for the first pillar prompt before you begin.
```

## Step 3 — Build the brain

### Pillar 1 — Positioning
```
Pillar 1 — Positioning.

Using the audience document and the mission and beliefs document in project
knowledge, draft my Positioning pillar.

Cover four things in this order:
1. Who it's for — be specific enough that the wrong person would self-select out.
2. What I stand for — three to five core stances, written as opinions not slogans.
3. What I'm against — name the patterns, beliefs, or behaviours I refuse to align with.
4. The one-line positioning statement that captures all of the above.

Format: narrative explanation at the top, structured rules at the bottom.
Be opinionated. If something in my raw material is weak, push back.
```

### Pillar 2 — Voice
```
Pillar 2 — Voice.

Reference the positioning document we just saved, plus the voice samples in
project knowledge.

Draft my Voice pillar. Cover:
1. Tone — three to five attributes, each with a one-sentence definition.
2. Rhythm and structure — how my sentences move. Pull from the voice samples.
3. Vocabulary — words I use, words I'd never use.
4. Examples — rewrite three generic creator-content sentences in my voice
   to demonstrate the rules in action.

Same format as Pillar 1: narrative top, structured rules bottom.
```

### Pillar 3 — Visual Identity
```
Pillar 3 — Visual Identity.

Reference the positioning and voice documents, plus the visual reference
in project knowledge.

Draft my Visual Identity pillar. Cover:
1. Palette — primary, secondary, accent. Hex codes. When each one is used.
2. Typography — display, body, accent. With usage rules.
3. Composition principles — how things sit on a page or a thumbnail.
4. Motifs — recurring visual elements that signal the brand.
5. What this isn't — visual choices I refuse.

Then output a complete HTML brand board in a single artifact:
palette swatches, type samples, composition example, principles, and
the what-this-isn't section. Use the brand's actual colours and fonts.
```

### Pillar 4 — Story
```
Pillar 4 — Story.

Reference all three pillars built so far, plus the story document in
project knowledge.

Draft my Story pillar. Cover:
1. Origin — the short version. Three or four sentences.
2. The shift — what changed, why it changed, and what I learned.
3. The through-line — one sentence that everything else hangs from.
4. Receipts — two or three concrete proof points that the through-line is real.

Then write three short pieces — a bio, an about-page paragraph, and an
Instagram intro — each pulling on the through-line, each in my voice.
```

### The lock prompt
```
Lock the brain.

All four pillars are now in project knowledge: Positioning, Voice,
Visual Identity, Story.

From this point on, every output you generate inside this project pulls
from all four. No exceptions.

Confirm the brain is locked, list the four pillars, and then prove it
by writing a single Instagram caption announcing a new piece of content.
Pull from positioning, voice, visual hints, and story — all four —
without me telling you which to use.
```

## Step 4 — Use the brain

Five things to call back into the brain: thumbnail decisions ("Does this fit the visual identity? Push back if not."), caption drafts ("Rewrite in voice. Tighten."), sponsorship evaluation, video angles ("Three angles that sit inside positioning, framed in voice."), bio rewrites.

Maintenance: paste strong writing back in and ask if it belongs in the voice samples. Every six months, run the lock prompt again. Don't rebuild the brain when something feels off. Edit the pillar. The system is the asset.

> Brand once. Distribute everywhere.
